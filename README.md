# About the Game

I started making this game bit by bit during my classes at school. I wanted to make my own FNaF-style game, but instead of going all out with complicated 3D stuff, I wanted to see if I could do it the old-school way, similar to how Scott Cawthon made the original FNaF games.

At first, I tried making it in **Clickteam Fusion**, since that's what the original FNaF games were made with. Unfortunately, I quickly realized that Fusion was pretty difficult for me to work with and felt outdated, so I eventually moved the project over to **Godot**.

The funny part is that moving to Godot basically meant learning everything from scratch again.

One of the coolest things I learned while making this was how Scott was able to make **PNG images look like they were part of a 3D environment**. I experimented with positioning, scaling, layering, and other techniques to make 2D images give the illusion of depth without actually building everything in 3D.

I also experimented with a bunch of other things for the menu.

## Stuff I Managed to Make

* **VHS-style menu animation** — I used multiple PNGs and animations to make the menu feel like an old VHS recording instead of just having a static screen.
* **Twitching entity** — My entity, which is basically me, can twitch and move in an unnatural way to make it feel more unsettling.
* **Custom button cursor** — When your mouse gets close to a button, the `>>` / `<<` design automatically moves to the button closest to your cursor.
* **Looping global audio** — The background audio automatically loops instead of stopping after one play.
* **Fullscreen support** — Press `F11` to toggle fullscreen.
* **Quick exit** — Press `ESC` to quit the game.
* **PNG-based visuals** — Most of the visuals and animations are built around PNG assets.
* **Sprites made in Canva** — I edited and prepared most of my sprites and visual assets using Canva before bringing them into Godot.

## So... Why Is It Unfinished?

Honestly, school happened.

I'm working on this completely solo, and I was basically developing the game whenever I had some free time between classes and schoolwork. Making something that looks 3D using mostly PNGs is already pretty demanding, and doing it while juggling school made it even harder.

Because of that, I wasn't able to finish the entire game before the final cut. What I managed to finish was mainly the **menu, its animations, and several of the small systems and mechanics behind it**.

Even though it's unfinished, I'm still pretty happy with what I managed to learn from it. I got to experiment with **Godot, PNG-based animation, fake 3D techniques, UI interactions, audio looping, visual effects, and game development in general**.

This project was basically me learning as I went, making things whenever I had the time, breaking stuff, fixing it, and occasionally wondering why I decided to make a FNaF game in the first place.

Still, it was a fun project to work on, and hopefully I can come back to it someday and actually finish the thing.
